Amazon Spectral Herbarium Network

A spectral herbarium is training

We train people across the Amazon to read the light reflected by leaves in botanical collections, building herbaria where every specimen keeps not only its shape but also its spectral signature.

How it works

From specimen to spectrum, in seconds

Amazonian herbaria hold decades of specimens identified by specialists. These collections can gain a new layer of information without cutting a single leaf.

Step 1 / 6

Choose the specimen

In the herbarium, dried plants are kept in cabinets, organized by family and species. We choose a specimen that has already been identified by specialists.

Cabinet → folder → specimen
Step 2 / 6

Record the sample

We scan the specimen's barcode, which works as its identity, and record the reading details (who measured, which device, which leaf and side) in the metadata protocol spreadsheet.

Metadata protocol · herbflow
Step 3 / 6

Calibrate the device

Before starting, the spectrometer measures a white reference piece. That way, a reading taken in Manaus can be compared with one taken in Quito or La Paz.

White reference
Step 4 / 6

Measure the leaf

We place the spectrometer on the leaf, with a black background underneath. It shines infrared light and measures how much of it comes back. We repeat this at several points, on both sides of the leaf.

No cutting, no damage
Step 5 / 6

See the spectrum

Within seconds, the reading becomes a curve on the screen: the spectral signature of that leaf. Faulty readings are redone on the spot.

900–1700 nm
Step 6 / 6

Store and share

The spectra, together with the data of each specimen, go into a standardized database, the same in every herbarium of the network. With them we train models that help recognize species.

Database · species identification

What we do

Training people is at the heart of the project

Technology only transforms a collection when there are people ready to use it. That is why every stage of HerbSpectra-Amazon begins and ends with training.

Workshops in the herbaria

Three to five days of hands-on practice with the spectrometer and the international protocol, inside each network herbarium.

Online courses

The fundamentals of spectroscopy applied to botanical collections, in Portuguese and Spanish, open to the whole region.

A shared protocol

Every herbarium measures its specimens the same way, so the data can be combined into a single collection.

A spectral database

Spectra linked to the name, locality and herbarium of each specimen form a standardized database for research, teaching and conservation.
